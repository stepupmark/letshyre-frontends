Say you run hiring at a 150-person fintech in Gurugram. You hire a senior backend engineer whose
CV shows three years at a consultancy in Noida. He joins on a Monday. The verification report
arrives ten days later: the consultancy's website was registered eight months ago, and the number
on his experience letter is answered by the same person who gave his reference.

The check worked. It just finished after the joining date, and the one person it asked had been
paid to say yes. Most fake experience gets through for those two reasons, and both are fixable. Run
the check before the start date, and verify the employer as well as the letter.

## Employment is the check that fails most

In the same report, address checks failed 7.68% of the time and education 4.49%. Most employment
discrepancies fall into a few patterns.

- **An employer that doesn't exist**, or exists only to issue letters.
- **Stretched dates**, to hide a gap or turn 18 months into three years.
- **A better title or salary** than the person held.
- **A job left off**, often a short one that ended badly.
- **Two jobs at once**, which an offer letter's exclusivity clause usually forbids.

## Start the check at acceptance, not at joining

Most companies send the <Term id="background-verification">background verification</Term> forms
in the joining kit. By then the candidate has resigned, and you've closed the role. A bad report
leaves you choosing between a messy exit and pretending you didn't read it.

Send the consent form the day the offer is accepted, and make the offer conditional on a
satisfactory report. A 60 or 90-day <Term id="notice-period">notice period</Term> gives the checks
time to finish. For immediate joiners, run the employment check first and the rest after.

## Verify the employer, not just the letter

A forged letter on a real company's letterhead is caught by a call to that company's HR. A real
letter from a fake company passes, because the phone number on the letter reaches the forger.

- **Find the company yourself.** Look it up on the Ministry of Corporate Affairs register, and check
  that it was incorporated before the candidate says they joined.
- **Call the number on its public listing**, not the one on the letter, and ask for HR, not the
  manager the candidate named.
- **Check that it looks like an employer.** A website, a public address and staff on LinkedIn who
  aren't all connected to the candidate.
- **Compare the PF record.** With consent, the candidate's <Term id="uan">UAN</Term> history shows
  which employers paid provident fund contributions and for which months.

Read the PF record with care. Firms with fewer than 20 employees aren't required to run EPF, so
missing contributions from a small company is a question to ask, not proof. A month of overlap at
a job change is normal. Six months of contributions from two employers isn't.

## Don't treat "no record" as a lie

Companies close, merge and rename. HR teams don't answer. Candidates write the job title they
were given, which may differ from the grade in the HR system. If you reject everyone whose report
says "unable to verify", you'll lose good people for their old employer's paperwork.

Ask the candidate to explain before you decide, and give them a few days to send documents:
payslips, a <Term id="relieving-letter">relieving letter</Term>, Form 16, bank statements showing
salary credits. Most innocent mismatches clear in a day. Fake ones rarely produce a consistent set
of all four. We've written [the candidate's side](/en/blogs/bgv-mismatch) of the same process,
which you can share with anyone who's flagged.

## Get consent for each check, in writing

India's data protection rules, notified in November 2025, bring their consent and notice duties
into force by May 2027. Most verification firms already ask for signed consent, but the form is
yours. List each check you'll run, who will run it, and how long you'll keep the report. Don't
collect more than the role needs, and don't run checks you wouldn't act on.

## The interview catches what paperwork can't

A real company and a real letter still don't prove the person can do the work they describe. Two
or three questions about the systems they built, asked the same way to every candidate in a

<Term id="structured-interview">structured interview</Term>, show whether three
years of experience is three years deep.

For the fintech in Gurugram, the fix is a change of date and one search. The next senior hire
signs the consent form the day they accept. The verification report arrives five weeks before they
join, and someone looks up each employer before calling it.
