Say you're hiring a frontend developer for a SaaS company in Pune. You post the role from your JD,
open the candidate pool, and there are 38 people under AI matched, every one of them already
interviewed. You have ten free credits.

You could open the ten best matches and see what happens. You'd spend every credit and learn less than you
think. Each card in the pool already tells you six things, free, and they're enough to get to the
four people worth a closer look. The trick is reading them together.

## The six things on every card

Each number answers a different question.

- **Match** is how well the person fits this job overall, from the interview and the resume. The
  list is sorted by it.
- **Role fit** says whether the role they interviewed for and their background line up with yours.
- **Skills fit** shows which of your job's skills they have, and which they're missing.
- **Interview score**, out of 100, is how they performed in the AI interview. It measures the
  interview, not the fit.
- **Experience** comes from their profile. People without work experience show as freshers.
- **Interviews** is how many AI interviews they've taken. Each one is unlocked separately.

## Read them in pairs, not one at a time

A single number misleads. Pairs tell a story.

- **High match, modest interview score.** Usually the right background on a weaker day. Worth a
  look if the missing skills are ones you can teach.
- **High interview score, partial match.** Someone strong whose background is a step away from your
  role. Check which skills are missing before you rule them out.
- **Strong role fit, one key skill missing.** If that skill is the one the job lives on, move on. If
  it's a framework they could pick up in a month, keep them.

Open a candidate before you spend anything. You'll see why the AI matched them: the role fit, the
job's skills they have and don't, their experience and their interviews.

## From 38 to four

Here's how the Pune team gets to a shortlist without a credit spent.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Filter to Strong">
    Match strength filters to Strong, Partial or Low. Start with Strong, and
    come back to Partial only if the list is thin.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the skill your job can't do without">
    For this role it's React with TypeScript. Anyone missing it drops off,
    however good the rest looks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Look at experience against the band you need">
    The JD says four to seven years. A strong eleven-year profile may want a
    different job, and a two-year one a different budget.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the pairs">
    Keep the strong matches with good interview scores, and one or two strong
    backgrounds with a weaker score. That's often where the best value is.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Then switch from AI matched to Applied. The people who applied are ranked the same way, and someone
who chose your job is often more likely to take it.

## Choose which interview to open

When you unlock, you choose which of the candidate's interviews to open, and each costs one credit.
The interview closest to your job's role is marked. Start there; if a candidate has interviewed for
three roles, you rarely need all three.

Before you confirm, we show the cost and what your balance will be. An unlock can't be undone, and
what you unlock stays unlocked on your account, under Unlocked candidates.

<Callout tone="tip">
  If a candidate's match is strong but you're unsure, share the decision rather
  than the credit. Your teammates on the plan can review the same pool with you.
</Callout>

## What the Pune team spends

Four candidates, four interviews closest to the role, four credits. Six left for the next role, and
four scorecards and recordings to read before anyone picks up the phone. We've written about
[what to do with those recordings](/en/blogs/watch-the-recording) before the first call.

The [employer guide](/en/employer-guide) walks through every screen, from verifying your company to
sharing a scorecard.
