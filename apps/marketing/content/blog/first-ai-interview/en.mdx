Say you're Kavya, a data analyst in Hyderabad, and you've set aside Saturday morning for your
first LetsHyre interview. Coffee's made. You open the desktop app, click Take Interview, and the
security check stops you: Chrome is still open in the background, with the job post you were
reading last night.

Nothing went wrong yet, but that's how most bad interview days start. Not with a hard question,
with a browser left open, a headset swapped after the voice sample, or a phone face-down on the
desk. Know the steps before the first question and you spend the interview on the questions.

## The night before

Everything you need is on the computer you'll use and in the room you'll use it in.

<Checklist title="The night before your interview">
  <Check>
    A Windows computer with the LetsHyre desktop app installed and signed in
  </Check>
  <Check>
    A clear, recent profile photo on your account; the app compares you with it
  </Check>
  <Check>
    Camera and microphone working, and the headset you'll use plugged in
  </Check>
  <Check>A quiet room, lit from the front, with a door you can close</Check>
  <Check>Your phone and any second screen out of view</Check>
  <Check>The steadiest internet connection you have</Check>
</Checklist>

Interviews run only on Windows for now, not on a phone, tablet or Mac. The
[candidate guide](/en/candidate-guide) has the full list of requirements.

## Four checks before the first question

When you click Take Interview, the app runs four checks. They're there to make the interview fair
for everyone, and each one is quick if you're ready for it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Close other apps">
    The app looks for anything that could get in the way: web browsers, screen
    recorders, AI assistants and video-call tools. Close whatever it flags.
    Doing it before you click saves a restart.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Allow camera, microphone and screen">
    The interview is supervised through all three, so allow each when Windows
    asks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Match your face to your photo">
    The camera takes a live photo and compares it with your profile photo. Face
    the light.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record your voice sample">
    You read a short statement aloud, and your spoken answers are checked
    against it. Record it in the same room, with the same microphone, you'll
    answer with.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The voice sample catches more people than any other step. If you record it on the laptop's
built-in microphone and then plug in a headset, your answers may not match it, and an answer that
doesn't match won't let you move on.

## Choose the role, then read the rules

Each interview is for one role. The role you picked during onboarding is already selected, and you
can keep it or choose another. Choose carefully: you can take up to three interviews in total, and
every attempt counts. We've written about
[how to spend all three](/en/blogs/three-interviews-roles).

Then a "Before you begin" screen lists what the proctoring watches for. Read it properly, because
these are the rules that can end an interview.

- **Strikes** come from leaving the interview window or full screen, someone else in view or
  nobody in view, a phone in view, or apps and extra screens the app blocks. Three strikes end the
  interview, and your answers are submitted.
- **Warnings** come from your face briefly leaving the frame, poor light or the camera pausing.
  They ask you to fix something and don't count against you.
- **Internet drops** aren't strikes, but three of them submit the interview.

## Answering the questions

The timer starts as soon as the interview opens. Each question has its own time limit, and you can
move between questions until the session ends. Questions can be multiple choice, pseudocode, coding,
written or spoken, depending on the role.

A few habits help with every type.

- **Read the whole question before you start**, especially the coding and written ones. Answering
  the question you expected is the commonest way to lose marks.
- **For spoken answers, think for a moment, then talk in order**: what you'd do, why, and what you'd
  check. The scorecard looks at clarity, relevance, confidence, depth and the key ideas you cover.
- **Skip and come back** if a question stalls you. The session timer is the one that matters.
- **Stay in the window.** Don't reach for your phone to check anything, even the time.

<Callout tone="tip">
  Talk about your own work wherever the question allows. A real example from a
  project you shipped is clearer, and harder to mistake for a memorised answer,
  than a textbook definition.
</Callout>

## After you submit

Your answers and recording upload as you go, so keep the connection steady to the end. A few
minutes after you finish, your scorecard appears in your dashboard under Scorecards.

From there it works for you. We match you to open roles as they're posted, usually within a day,
and employers hiring for them find you in their candidate pool. You can also apply to jobs
yourself, and follow each application from Applied to Shortlisted and Selected.

When an employer unlocks your interview, they see your contact details, documents, full scorecard
and recording, and contact you directly. We don't send a notification for that yet, so keep your
phone on and check your email.

Kavya closes Chrome, plugs in her headset before the voice sample, and turns her desk lamp to face
