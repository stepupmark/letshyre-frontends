Ask ten people how the Indian job market is doing and you'll get ten answers, mostly based on their
own last job search. The data tells a more specific story. Hiring is growing, but not evenly, and
the unevenness is the interesting part.

Here's what the most recent numbers say, what they don't, and what to do about it on either side of
the table.

## The headline: employers want to hire

ManpowerGroup asks employers every quarter whether they expect to add or cut staff. Its measure, the
net employment outlook, is the share planning to hire minus the share planning to cut. For the last
quarter of 2026, India's reading was unusually strong.

Intentions aren't hires, though. For what's actually happening, the best monthly signal is Naukri's
JobSpeak index, which tracks new white-collar job listings.

## Experience is where the growth is

The steadiest month to read is
[September](https://www.naukri.com/blog/naukri-jobspeak-october-2026-white-collar-hiring-ai-ml-growth/), before festive-season effects muddy the comparison.
Overall hiring was up just 2% on a year earlier. Split by experience, the picture changes.

Early and mid-career hiring was flat or slightly down. Everyone with eight or more years of
experience was above the average. October's numbers, published in early November, pointed the same
way and more sharply: listings for people with 13 to 16 years and with 16 or more years each rose
[22% on a year earlier](https://remunance.com/industry-news/naukri-jobspeak-october-2026-white-collar-hiring/).

The data doesn't say why, but one explanation fits. Companies are hiring people who can run things
now, rather than people they'll need to train. Some of the routine work that used to fill a
junior's first year is also going to AI tools, which need an experienced person to direct and check
them.

<Callout>
  October's overall growth of 14% looks dramatic, but part of it is a weak
  comparison. Last October, festive-season holidays slowed hiring, so this
  year's month is measured against a dip. September's 2% is the more sober
  number.
</Callout>

## It isn't the IT boom you remember

For two decades, "hiring in India" mostly meant software. The October data says the growth has
moved.

[Production and manufacturing listings rose 39%](https://www.careerindia.com/news/india-job-market-2026-ai-ml-hiring-surge-october-report-011-68141.html)
and automobile 36%, both far ahead of the average. Global capability centres, the in-house tech and
operations hubs that multinationals run in India, grew 18%. General software and QA grew 1%.

The one part of tech that is booming is AI. Listings for AI and machine learning roles rose 32% in
October, after 20% in September. That's not more software engineers. It's a different kind of
engineer.

By city, Hyderabad led October at 15%, then Pune at 14% and Delhi-NCR at 13%. Bengaluru and Mumbai
grew 12%, Chennai 11%, and Kolkata was flat.

## If you're hiring

The people in demand are the people everyone else wants too. That changes how you should hire them.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Plan for the notice period">
    Senior people in India often serve 60 to 90 days of notice. Start
    conversations before the role is urgent, and treat someone already serving
    their notice as a serious advantage, not a footnote.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Don't make senior people re-prove the basics">
    Experienced candidates have options. A process that puts a 16-year engineer
    through three rounds of screening calls will lose them to a company that
    gets to the real conversation faster.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check judgement, not just skills">
    You're hiring them to make calls you can't. Ask for one real decision they
    made, what they knew at the time, and what they'd change.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Look beyond the usual cities">
    Hyderabad and Pune grew faster than Bengaluru in October. If your hiring
    plan still assumes one city, the people you need may be elsewhere.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## If you're looking

- **Experience is selling. Make it visible.** The market is rewarding people who can show they've
  run things. Lead with the systems, teams and decisions you owned, with numbers, not with years.
- **AI skills are a premium, not a requirement, yet.** The fastest-growing listings are for AI and
  machine learning. Even outside those roles, being able to say how you use AI in your own work, and
  where you don't trust it, is becoming a useful thing to have an answer to.
- **Look at where hiring is growing, not where it used to.** Manufacturing, automobile and global
  capability centres are hiring tech, finance and operations people. If you've only been searching
  for "software company", you're searching a flat market.
- **If you're serving notice, say so early.** Employers trying to fill senior roles quickly will
  move faster for someone who can join in weeks rather than months.

## What to watch next

ManpowerGroup's next survey, covering the first quarter of 2027, is due in December. Naukri's
November index will show whether October's jump was mostly the festive base or something more
lasting. If senior hiring keeps outrunning junior hiring into 2027, it's no longer a blip. It's how
companies have decided to grow.

Put both dates in your calendar. Whether you're planning next quarter's hiring or your own search,
plan it around those two numbers rather than what the last ten people told you.
