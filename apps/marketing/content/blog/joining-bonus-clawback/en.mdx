Say you're Sana, a product designer in Gurugram. In December you accepted an offer of ₹24 lakh with a
₹2 lakh <Term id="joining-bonus">joining bonus</Term>, paid with your first salary in January. Page four of the
offer letter had one sentence you skimmed: "If you resign within 12 months of joining, you will
repay the joining bonus in full."

In October a better role comes along. HR does the maths for you: ₹2 lakh, taken from your final
settlement. But only ₹1.48 lakh of that bonus ever reached your account. The rest went in tax, in a
financial year that has already closed. A clawback clause can cost you more than the bonus paid you,
and the time to change it is before you sign.

## Three clauses that look alike and aren't

Most joining bonus clauses in Indian offers are one of three kinds. They read almost the same on the
page, and they cost very different amounts if you leave early.

- **Full repayment.** Leave one day before the date and you repay all of it. This is Sana's clause.
- **Pro-rata repayment.** You repay the share of the period you didn't serve. Leave after nine of 12
  months and you repay a quarter.
- **Split payment.** Half at joining, half at six months, with nothing to repay. You can lose the
  second half, but you never owe money.

The same pattern turns up beyond joining bonuses. Relocation allowances, a reimbursed <Term
id="notice-buyout">notice buyout</Term> and training bonds often come with a repayment clause.
Search the offer letter and its annexures for "recover", "repay" and "refund" before you sign.

## Why you pay back more than you got

A joining bonus is taxed as salary in the month it's paid. On a ₹24 lakh <Term id="ctc">CTC</Term>, a ₹2 lakh bonus is
taxed on top of the year's pay, at 25% plus cess, so about ₹52,000 goes to tax first.

If you repay it in the same financial year, the employer can usually reverse it in payroll. The
bonus drops out of your income, and the tax comes back through your final settlement or your
return. Sana's problem is the calendar. The bonus came in January, in the 2025-26 year. The
repayment comes in October, in 2026-27.

Once the year has closed, getting that tax back is [much
harder](https://zdblogs.zohocorp.com/payroll/academy/in/taxes-and-compliances/tax-on-bonus.html).
Employers rarely revise an old year's Form 16, which is called Form 130 from the 2026-27 tax year.
Without that, the ₹52,000 is usually gone, and Sana repays ₹2 lakh for a bonus that paid her ₹1.48
lakh.

## Can a company actually enforce it?

Usually, yes. In May 2025 the Supreme Court upheld a clause making a bank officer pay ₹2 lakh for
leaving before three years. It said a condition that applies while you're employed is [not a
restraint of
trade](https://ssrana.in/articles/revisiting-employment-bond-enforceability-in-india-vijaya-bank-vs-prashant-b-narnaware/).
That case was about a public-sector bank, and lawyers still argue over how far it reaches. Courts
also look at whether the amount is reasonable compared with what the employer lost.

In practice, most companies don't need a court. They take the amount from your final settlement
before it's paid, so you'll argue from a weaker position than you had on the day you signed.

## What to ask for before you sign

Recruiters change these clauses more often than candidates expect, because the bonus is already
budgeted. Ask for one or two of these, in writing.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pro-rata instead of full">
    The most useful change. Leaving at month nine costs a quarter of the bonus
    instead of all of it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repay what reached you, not the gross">
    Ask for repayment of the amount after tax, or a commitment that payroll will
    reverse it in the same year.
  </Step>
  <Step title="No repayment if the company lets you go">
    Make sure the clause applies only when you resign. If your role is cut, you
    shouldn't owe anything.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pay it in two parts">
    Half at joining and half at six months. It's often easier for HR to agree to
    than a change to the clause itself.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Before you sign the offer

Find the clause, and note three things: the period, full or pro-rata, and the month the bonus will
be paid. If it lands between January and March, a repayment almost certainly falls in a
different financial year. That's when the gross-versus-net question matters most. We go through the rest of
the letter in [negotiating your offer in India](/en/blogs/negotiate-offer-india).

Sana's clause would have cost the recruiter nothing to change in December. In October, it cost her
₹52,000 she never received.
