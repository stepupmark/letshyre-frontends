Say you're Meera, a data analyst in Pune. In October you accepted an offer of ₹18 lakh, the same <Term id="ctc">CTC</Term>
your friend Arjun signed for at the same company in 2024. You join, the first payslip arrives, and
it's ₹6,200 lower than his was in his first month.

Nobody cut Meera's pay. The company moved money around inside the same ₹18 lakh, because India's
four labour codes, in force since 21 November 2025, changed what counts as "wages". If you're
signing an offer in 2026, the CTC on the letter tells you less than it used to. The breakdown
underneath it tells you more.

## The rule is about allowances, not basic pay

You've probably read that "basic must now be 50% of CTC". That's the short version going around,
and it's not what the [Code on
Wages](https://www.mondaq.com/india/employee-benefits-compensation/1848470/wages-under-labour-codes-tipping-the-balance)
says.

Under the codes, wages means basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance. A list of
things is left out: HRA, conveyance, overtime, commission, bonus, the employer's PF and a few more.
The rule that changes offers is one line long. If the parts left out add up to more than half of
your total pay, the extra counts as wages anyway.

So the law doesn't set your basic. It stops a company from keeping basic at 30% and paying the rest
as allowances, because the extra comes back in for PF and gratuity either way. Most payroll teams
found it simpler to raise basic to about half and stop arguing over which allowance is which.

## Where Meera's ₹6,200 went

PF is 12% of wages, from you and from the employer. Gratuity is also worked out on wages. When basic
goes from 30% to 50% of <Term id="fixed-pay">fixed pay</Term>, both grow. If the CTC stays at ₹18 lakh, the employer's larger
share has to come out of somewhere, and that somewhere is your gross salary.

That ₹6,200 hasn't disappeared. Over a year it's about ₹74,000, and it now goes
into Meera's provident fund and her future gratuity instead of her account. Her tax bill even drops
by about ₹10,000, because her taxable salary is smaller. The money earns interest and she gets it
later. Whether that's a loss depends on whether she needed it this month, for rent or an EMI, or
would have saved it anyway.

Try your own offer. Change the basic share from 30 to 50 and watch the monthly figure.

## Five questions to ask before you sign

Recruiters rarely raise this. Ask in writing, and ask for the answers in the offer letter or its
salary annexure.

<Steps>
  <Step title="What is basic as a share of the total, and is it final?">
    Some companies restructured once and some are still doing it. If your
    breakdown is going to change after you join, you want to know now, not on
    your third payslip.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Is PF on my full basic, or on the ceiling?">
    Many employers pay PF only on the statutory wage ceiling. The Cabinet
    [raised that ceiling to ₹25,000 a
    month](https://newsonair.gov.in/govt-raises-epf-wage-ceiling-from-rs-15000-to-rs-25000-per-month/)
    in September 2026, so capped PF is about ₹3,000 a month each side. If PF
    stays capped, a bigger basic barely touches your take-home. Some companies
    let you pick.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Is gratuity inside the CTC?">
    If it is, a higher basic quietly moves more of your CTC into a payment you
    only get after five years. Ask what the CTC is with gratuity left out.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Is this a fixed-term contract?">
    Fixed-term employees now qualify for gratuity after one year of service,
    [not five](https://labour.gov.in/sites/default/files/pib2192463.pdf). That
    makes a one-year contract worth more than it was in 2024.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Does the company offer employer NPS?">
    Under the new tax regime, an employer's NPS contribution of up to 14% of
    basic and DA isn't taxed in your hands. A bigger basic raises that limit,
    which is one of the few ways to cut tax in the new regime.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Use the new structure in the negotiation

The breakdown gives you a better argument than "I want more". If two offers carry the same CTC and
one pays more each month, compare them on the monthly figure. We go through that in [negotiating your offer in
India](/en/blogs/negotiate-offer-india). If one of them adds a joining bonus, read [its repayment
clause](/en/blogs/joining-bonus-clawback) too.

costs nothing, because capping PF lowers the employer's share too.

<Callout tone="note">
  The central rules were notified in 2026, but labour is shared between the
  Centre and the states, and several states are still finalising theirs. The
  Ministry's FAQs [aren't binding
  law](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/04/26/ministry-faqs-four-labour-codes-india-2026/),
  and experts still disagree on edge cases, such as whether a special allowance
  counts as wages.
</Callout>

## One more change: you get paid faster when you leave

The same code says wages owed to someone who resigns must be paid within [two working
days](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/two-working-days-code-wages-employee-final-settlement-552780),
instead of the 30 to 60 days many companies used to take. That covers wages only, and gratuity has
its own 30-day rule. If you're serving a <Term id="notice-period">notice period</Term> now, it's
worth knowing before your last day.

## Before you reply to the offer

Ask for the salary annexure, not just the CTC. Put the basic share into the calculator above and
write down the monthly in-hand figure. If it's lower than what you take home today, raise it the way
Meera would: with the number, the reason, and a cheaper option for the company. In her case, that's
PF on the ceiling.
