Say you're Meera, a data analyst in Hyderabad. You joined your company on 1 July 2022, on a basic
of ₹60,000 a month. A new offer pays ₹4 lakh a year more and wants you to start on 1 January. You
accept on the call and resign the next morning.

What nobody mentions on that call is two dates. On 31 March your employer pays last year's bonus,
₹1.5 lakh for you, but only to people still on the rolls. On 30 June you complete five years and
qualify for <Term id="gratuity">gratuity</Term>, about ₹1.73 lakh. By leaving on 31 December, Meera
gives up ₹3.23 lakh, most of what her first year's raise was worth.

## Find the dates before you pick one

Most money tied to an employer pays out on a date, and you get it only if you're still there.
Before you agree a joining date, find each one.

- **Gratuity.** Five years of continuous service for permanent staff, one year on a fixed-term
  contract. It's 15 days of your last basic pay for each year, with a month counted as 26 days.
- **The annual bonus or <Term id="variable-pay">variable pay</Term>.** Policies usually pay only
  people on the rolls on the payout date, even if you worked the whole year.
- **Retention bonuses and deferred pay.** Often paid in instalments, each with its own date.
- **ESOPs.** Your next vesting date, and how long you have to exercise after you leave. We cover
  that in [startup ESOPs or more cash](/en/blogs/startup-esops-vs-cash).
- **Your joining bonus from this employer.** If you joined less than a year ago, check the
  [repayment clause](/en/blogs/joining-bonus-clawback) before you resign.

Gratuity is the one people miss most, because it often sits inside the <Term id="ctc">CTC</Term>
and looks like salary. Under five years, it's usually money you were never going to see. It's also
grown for many people since [the labour codes raised basic pay](/en/blogs/labour-codes-offer).

## Ask the new employer to cover the gap

Meera has three options, and only one of them is to wait.

Moving her start to 1 April keeps the bonus. Most employers can absorb a few weeks if you say why,
and it's a better reason than most they hear. Waiting until July for gratuity is a harder ask, and
some offers won't survive it.

The third option is to ask the new employer to pay what she's giving up, as a

<Term id="joining-bonus">joining bonus</Term>. That's the normal use for one.

A number and a document get further than a feeling. Offer to share the bonus policy or last year's
payslip. If they agree, read the clawback terms, because you'll usually owe it back if you leave
within a year.

## Transfer your PF, don't withdraw it

When you move, your provident fund should move with you under the same UAN, the number that
follows you between employers. Log in to the EPFO member portal after your first month and check
that the old balance arrived.

Withdrawing instead is tempting and usually expensive. Money taken out before five years of
continuous service is taxed, and transfers count your years at both employers as one run. Under the
EPFO's newer rules, people out of work can take up to 75% at once, but the rest waits 12 months and
the pension part 36, as the Labour Ministry
[clarified in October 2025](https://scroll.in/latest/1087719/centre-clarifies-75-of-provident-fund-can-be-withdrawn-immediately-after-leaving-job).

## Use your leave, or get it paid

Earned leave you haven't taken is usually paid in your

<Term id="full-and-final-settlement">full and final settlement</Term>, at basic
or gross pay depending on the policy. Sick and casual leave usually aren't. Read
your leave policy before you resign, because some companies don't let you take
leave during the
<Term id="notice-period">notice period</Term>, and others cap what they'll pay.

The Code on Wages now says wages due must be paid within two working days of leaving. Many
companies still settle in 30 to 45 days. Ask HR for the date and a written breakup on your last day.

## Tell the new employer what you've earned this year

Your new employer deducts tax as if their salary were your only income. When two employers each
give you the lower slabs, you end up owing tax in July. Give the new payroll team your earlier
salary and the tax already deducted in your first month, and keep the old employer's Form 16.

Meera's list has two lines that matter. She asks for a 1 April start, explains the bonus, and the
new employer agrees. Gratuity she gives up on purpose, knowing the number, which is a different
decision from losing it by accident.
