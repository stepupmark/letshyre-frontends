The recruiter calls with good news. "We're offering 20 lakhs." You do some quick maths, divide by
twelve, and start picturing a monthly salary of about ₹1.66 lakh.

Then the first payslip arrives, and it's nowhere near that.

Nobody lied to you. <Term id="ctc">CTC</Term>, cost to company, is exactly what it says: what you cost the company in a
year. It's not what you earn in a year, and it's certainly not what lands in your account. Before you
negotiate anything, you need to know which parts of the number are yours.

## What's inside a CTC number

Every company builds its offer differently, but most Indian CTCs are made of the same four kinds of
money. Only one of them is cash you can count on every month.

- **<Term id="fixed-pay">Fixed pay</Term>** is your basic salary plus allowances like HRA. This is the monthly money, before
  your own PF contribution, professional tax and income tax come out.
- **<Term id="variable-pay">Variable pay</Term>** depends on your performance, the company's, or both. It might pay in full, in
  part, or not at all.
- **Employer PF** is real money, but it goes into your provident fund account, not your bank.
- **Gratuity** is usually only paid if you stay at least five years. Many people never see it from a
  given employer.

<Callout tone="note">
  India's four labour codes took effect on 21 November 2025, and many employers
  have been [reworking salary
  structures](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/tax/story/labour-codes-2025-how-50-wage-rule-would-alter-salaries-tax-outgo-retirement-savings-508334-2025-12-27)
  so that basic pay is a bigger share of the total. A larger basic raises PF and
  gratuity, which can lower take-home pay for the same CTC. Ask for the full
  breakdown, not just the total.
</Callout>

## Compare offers on fixed pay, not CTC

Two offers with the same CTC can be very different jobs to live on. One might be ₹20 lakh with ₹2
lakh variable. Another might be ₹20 lakh with ₹5 lakh variable, a joining bonus counted in, and an
employer PF calculated on a higher basic. The second one looks the same on paper and pays noticeably
less every month.

<Compare

  prefer="Offer A is 16.3 lakh fixed with a 2 lakh variable that paid out in full last year. Offer B is 15.5 lakh fixed with a 4 lakh variable and a one-time 1.5 lakh joining bonus. A pays more every month."

When you ask about variable pay, ask one question that most candidates skip: how much of it actually
paid out last year? A target bonus that's paid at 60% is worth 60% of what the letter says.

## Before you negotiate, know your numbers

The candidates who negotiate well aren't more confident. They're better prepared. Three numbers do
most of the work, and the same ones answer [the salary expectations
question](/en/blogs/salary-expectations) when it comes on the first call.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Your current fixed pay">
    Not your current CTC. Recruiters often anchor on the CTC you share, so know
    what you're actually paid and say both if asked.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your expected fixed pay, with a reason">
    "I'm looking for 18 lakh fixed, based on the scope of this role and what
    similar roles are paying" lands better than a percentage hike pulled from
    habit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your walk-away number">
    The fixed pay below which you'd stay where you are. Decide it before the
    call, not during it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What to ask for besides the salary

When the budget for fixed pay is genuinely tight, there's often room elsewhere. These are worth
asking about, especially in senior roles.

<Figure
  src={asks}
  srcDark={asksDark}
  alt="A checklist of six things to ask about besides salary, in two columns. Money, a joining bonus, notice buyout support and a guaranteed first-year variable. Terms, the job title and level, a review date in six months and remote or hybrid days. Each item has an empty checkbox"
  width={720}
  height={288}
  caption="Ask for one or two, not all six. The ones that matter most to you are the ones worth spending goodwill on."
/>

- **A joining bonus** can close the gap when the fixed-pay band is fixed. Ask whether you'd have to
  pay it back if you leave within a year.
- **<Term id="notice-buyout">Notice buyout</Term> support** matters if you're serving a long notice. We've written about [how
  buyouts work](/en/blogs/notice-period-job-hunt).
- **A guaranteed first-year variable** protects you from a target you had no part in setting.
- **Title and level** decide your next raise and your next job. They're often easier to move than
  the salary.
- **An early review date**, such as six months in, with the criteria written down.
- **Working arrangements** like remote days, which have a real cash value if they save you a long
  commute.

## How to have the conversation

Asking is normal. Once a company has decided to make you an offer, a polite and specific request is
unlikely to make them take it back.

- **Be warm about the role first.** "I'm excited about this, and I'd like to make the numbers work"
  sets the tone for everything after it.
- **Ask for one specific thing.** "Could you get the fixed pay to 18 lakh?" is easier to say yes to
  than "Can you do better?"
- **Get it in writing.** A verbal promise about a bonus, a review or a buyout is not part of your
  offer until it's in the letter.
- **Don't invent a competing offer.** Recruiters talk to each other more than you'd think, and a
  bluff that gets called can end the conversation.

  This post is general guidance, not tax or legal advice. How your pay is taxed
  depends on your regime and your full salary structure. If an offer is complex,
  ask the employer's HR team to walk you through the payslip.
</Callout>

## Start from a stronger position

The easiest negotiations are the ones where the employer already knows what you're worth before the
first call. On LetsHyre, your profile carries your current and expected CTC and your <Term id="notice-period">notice period</Term>,
so the employers who reach out already know where you stand.

Before the next recruiter call, work out three numbers: your fixed pay per month now, the lowest
monthly in-hand you'd accept, and the CTC that gets you there. The calculator above
works out the last one. When someone says "20 lakhs", you'll
already know what the payslip looks like.
