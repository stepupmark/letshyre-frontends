Say you're Divya, a product manager in Chennai. At 9:40 on a Thursday night, your phone buzzes. The
recruiter, on WhatsApp: "Hi Divya! Great news, we'd like to offer you 28L CTC 🎉 Can you confirm by
tomorrow EOD?"

You want to say yes, and you want to ask for more, and you're in your pyjamas. Most offer
negotiations in India now start like this, in a chat app, late in the day, with a deadline. The
words you send in the next 24 hours matter more than any confidence you bring to a call. Here are
the messages, one for each step, and the reasons behind them.

## Message one: thank them, and move it to email

Don't negotiate in the chat. WhatsApp is fast and friendly, which is exactly why it's a bad place to
discuss money. Numbers get lost between stickers, there's no subject line to find later, and a
thumbs-up can read as a yes.

> Thank you, that's great news and I'm really glad. Could you email me the offer letter with the
> full salary breakup? I'll go through it carefully and come back to you by Monday.

Two things are happening here. You're warm, which sets the tone for everything after. And you've
set your own deadline, close enough to be reasonable, without arguing with theirs.

## Message two: when they push for tomorrow

Short deadlines are common and usually soft. A recruiter who has spent four weeks on a hire rarely
loses it over a weekend.

> I understand you'd like to close this quickly, and so would I. I want to give the offer the
> attention it deserves, so Monday is the earliest I can respond properly. If that doesn't work for
> your timeline, please let me know.

If the answer is a hard no on any delay at all, treat it as information about how the company makes
decisions.

## Message three: when the breakup is missing

You can't negotiate a <Term id="ctc">CTC</Term> you can't see inside. Ask for the parts before you
respond to the total.

> Thanks for sending this. Before I respond, could you share the breakup: fixed pay, variable pay
> and how much of last year's variable paid out, PF, and whether gratuity or a joining bonus is
> counted in the 28L?

The question about last year's payout is the one most candidates skip. A variable paid at 60% is
worth 60% of what the letter says. Since the labour codes, the breakup also tells you how [the
same CTC can pay less each month](/en/blogs/labour-codes-offer).

## Message four: the counter

This is the one that matters. Send it by email, keep it short, and ask for one specific thing. Here
is Divya's, after the breakup showed ₹23.5 lakh fixed and ₹4.5 lakh <Term id="variable-pay">variable</Term>.

> Subject: Offer for Senior Product Manager, Divya R.
>
> Hi Ritu,
>
> Thank you for the offer. I'm excited about the role, and about the payments roadmap in
> particular.
>
> Having gone through the breakup, I'd like to ask whether the fixed pay could move to 26 lakh. The
> role covers two product lines, and that's in line with what similar roles are paying in Chennai
> right now. If the fixed pay is hard to move, I'd be glad to talk about a joining bonus instead.
>
> I'd like to make this work, and I'm happy to get on a call if that's easier.
>
> Best,
> Divya

The second version names the number, names the part of the offer, and gives the recruiter a second
way to say yes. It doesn't mention a competing offer, because Divya doesn't have one. Don't invent
one.

## Message five: when they say it's the best they can do

Sometimes it is. A no on salary still leaves the rest of the offer open, so ask for one thing that
costs the company less.

> Thank you for checking, I appreciate it. If the fixed pay can't move, would it be possible to
> bring the first salary review forward to six months, with the criteria agreed now?

Pick the one that matters most to you. An early review, a title, remote days or [help with a notice
buyout](/en/blogs/notice-period-job-hunt) are the usual options. Asking for all of them reads as a
list, not a priority.

## Message six: accepting, in writing

Divya's recruiter came back on Tuesday with 25 lakh fixed and a 1.5 lakh <Term id="joining-bonus">joining bonus</Term>. When you've
agreed, confirm everything in one email, so nothing said on a call is lost.

> Hi Ritu, thank you for working through this with me. I'm happy to accept. To confirm what we
> agreed: fixed pay of 25 lakh, variable of 4.5 lakh, a joining bonus of 1.5 lakh, a salary review at
> six months, and a joining date of 2 February. Could you send the revised offer letter with these
> terms? I'll sign as soon as it arrives.

Don't resign from your current job until that revised letter is signed by both sides.

## Message seven: saying no, kindly

If you're turning the offer down, say so quickly and leave the door open. Recruiters move between
companies, and the one you decline today may call you about a better role in a year.

> Hi Ritu, thank you for the offer and for your time over the last few weeks. After a lot of
> thought, I've decided not to take it up. I've enjoyed every conversation with the team, and I'd be
> glad to stay in touch.

No reasons are needed. If you give one, keep it to a single sentence and make it about fit, not
about the offer being too low.
