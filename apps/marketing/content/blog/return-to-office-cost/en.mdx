Say you're Farhan, a data engineer in Bengaluru. You live in HSR Layout and your office is in
Manyata Tech Park. For two years you went in twice a week. The email on Monday says five days from
January, and your team lead says, "It's only three more days."

Three more days of a commute that takes about two and a half hours there and back. Farhan's pay
won't change, but his working week gets longer by more than seven hours, and he spends more on cabs
every month. Once you put both in numbers, you can decide what to do about them.

## The mandates are getting stricter

Indian IT companies have moved in the same direction at different speeds. One 2026 report says
[TCS asks many staff to come in five
days](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/indias-it-giants-tighten-return-to-office-rules-amid-ai-disruption-87216.htm)
a week, with attendance tied to variable pay. The same report says Wipro asks for three days of at
least six hours each, and Infosys counts office days per month. Product companies and GCCs vary
more, but the trend is the same.

The cost is worst in the cities where most of these jobs are.

## Put your commute in numbers

Count a full round trip, door to door, on a normal day, not your best one. Add what you spend: cab
or fuel, parking, and the lunch you'd have made at home.

<Tool name="commute-cost" />

At five days, 150 minutes and ₹350 a day, Farhan's commute takes about 54 hours and ₹7,600 a month.
That's roughly 81 working days a year, close to four months of working days, spent in traffic. At
two days it was 40% of that.

Farhan takes home about ₹95,000 a month, so ₹7,600 is what an 8% raise would add.
The new rule takes roughly that from him without touching his payslip.

## What you can ask for

Once you have the number, you can ask specific questions instead of complaining. Many teams have
more room than the email suggests.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Off-peak hours">
    Ask whether core hours allow you to travel before or after the rush. In
    Bengaluru, the same 10 km takes far longer at 6 pm than at 7 am.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A closer office">
    Large employers often have several campuses. A transfer to the one nearer
    home can be easier to agree than an exception to the rule.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Transport support">
    A company shuttle, a cab allowance or a parking pass. Some teams have these
    and don't advertise them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Which days">
    If the rule allows a choice, pick the days the team is actually in, so the
    trip is worth the time.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The first version is an opinion your manager can only agree or disagree with. The second gives them
a problem they can solve, and two ways to say yes.

## Count it when you compare offers

The same arithmetic changes how two offers compare. A ₹20 lakh offer with two office days near home
can be worth more than ₹22 lakh with five days across the city. When you look at a new role, ask
for the office policy and the office's address before you compare salaries. We go through the rest
of the comparison in [negotiating your offer in India](/en/blogs/negotiate-offer-india).

## Before January

Farhan should run his numbers this week and take them to his lead before the rule starts. People who
raise it early get the exceptions there are, and people who raise it in February are complaining
about a rule everyone else has adjusted to. If nothing changes and the commute stays at 81 working
days a year, that's a fair reason to look at roles closer to home.
