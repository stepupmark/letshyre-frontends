Say it's your first call with a recruiter for a role you want. Fifteen minutes in, after the
questions about your <Term id="notice-period">notice period</Term>, they ask: "And what are your salary expectations?" You say
"whatever's standard for the role", they say "sure", and a week later the offer comes in two lakh
below what you had in mind.

The fix is to arrive with a number. Work out the <Term id="fixed-pay">fixed pay</Term> you'd move for, say it once as a short
range, and steer the call back to the work. That takes ten minutes of preparation, and it changes
every conversation about money that follows.

## Why the question comes so early

Recruiters ask in the first call because they're checking one thing: whether you fit the band
they've been given. If you don't, neither of you wants to spend four rounds finding out. It's a
budget check, not a trap.

In India it usually arrives as two questions, current <Term id="ctc">CTC</Term> and expected CTC.
That matters. Your current number is already on the table, so dodging the expected one doesn't
keep your position hidden. The recruiter fills the blank with your current CTC plus whatever hike
is typical, and that becomes the anchor.

## Work out your number before the call

Start from what you live on, not from the headline figure. A CTC includes money that never reaches
your account each month, so two offers with the same CTC can feel very different by the tenth
payslip.

Check the range against the market too. Ask people in similar roles, read the bands in job ads that
publish them, and look at what the last two companies that approached you were offering. You're
looking for a range you can defend, not the highest number anyone has ever been paid.

## Say a range, in fixed pay

When the question comes, give the range once, say what it's based on and stop talking.

<Compare
  avoid="I'm flexible, whatever's standard for this role. I'm more interested in the opportunity."
  prefer="I'm looking at 18 to 20 lakh fixed, depending on how the variable and the rest of the package are structured. Can you share the band for this role?"
/>

Saying fixed pay out loud does two things. It stops the variable and the employer's PF being used
to make a lower offer look like a higher one, and it tells the recruiter you know how offers are
built. Asking for the band straight after turns a one-way question into a conversation.

Keep the bottom of your range at a number you'd accept. Recruiters tend to hear the lower end, and
an offer at the floor of what you said is hard to push back on.

## When they ask for your current CTC

Answer it plainly, split into fixed and variable, and don't round it up. Offer letters and payslips
get checked, and a padded number turns a salary conversation into a question about honesty.

If your current pay is low for the role, say why you're asking for more in the same breath:

- "My fixed pay now is 14 lakh. I'm looking for 18 because this role covers a team I'd be leading,
  which I'm not doing today."
- "I joined my current company during a hiring freeze, and my pay has lagged the market since. The
  range I gave is based on what similar roles are paying now."

The jump you're asking for needs a reason that sits in the new job, not just in the old one.
Check what it's worth in hand, too. Tax takes a bigger share of each extra rupee, so a 30% hike on
the CTC shows up as a smaller one on the payslip.

## When the band is below your range

Sometimes the recruiter tells you the band and it tops out under your floor. You have three moves,
and only one of them is to say yes.

1. **Ask what else moves.** A joining bonus can cover a
   [notice buyout](/en/blogs/notice-period-job-hunt), and a review after six months can close part
   of the gap. Ask whether the level itself is fixed.
2. **Ask whether the role could be scoped higher.** If you'd be doing more than the job description
   says, the band may belong to a smaller job.
3. **Walk away politely.** "Thanks for being straight with me. That's below what would make a move
   work for me, so let's not take up each other's time."

The third answer feels risky on a first call. It also tells you something useful, and recruiters
remember the people who were clear with them when the next role opens.

## Before your next recruiter call

Write three numbers where you'll see them during the call: the fixed pay you have now, the floor
you'd accept and the top of your range. When "and what are your salary expectations?" comes
fifteen minutes in, read the range off the note, ask for the band and get back to talking about
the work. The full negotiation comes later, and
[the offer guide](/en/blogs/negotiate-offer-india) covers what to ask for when it does.
