Picture the same Tuesday from two desks.

At one, a hiring manager opens a folder of resumes for a senior role. They all say "results-driven".
They all list the same frameworks. Somewhere in the pile are the three people who can actually do
the job, and the only way to find them is a week of phone screens.

At the other desk, one of those three people is on their fourth call of the week, answering
"so, tell me about yourself" again. Seven years of work, and it keeps getting reduced to keywords
and a first impression.

Both of them are losing time to the same gap. A resume tells you what someone claims. It can't
show you how they think. We built LetsHyre to close that gap, and this post walks through how it
works, what it costs, and where its limits are.

## A resume is a claim, not evidence

"Expert in React" might be true. It might mean one side project. The person reading it has no way
to tell, so they do the only thing they can: call everyone who looks plausible and find out.

LetsHyre moves that first conversation earlier and makes it the same for everyone. Before an
employer ever sees a candidate, the candidate has taken a role-based AI interview. The interview
produces a skill scorecard and a full recording, and LetsHyre combines the scorecard with an AI
reading of the resume to show how well the person fits a given job.

### If you're hiring

You post the job description, and LetsHyre reads the requirements out of it: the skills, the
experience, whether it's remote. The AI ranks the people who applied, and recommends matching
profiles from the talent pool who haven't applied yet. From there, the work that used to take a week
of calls becomes reading.

## What an employer sees before paying anything

This is the part we spent the longest on. If you have to pay to find out whether a candidate is any
good, you end up paying for a lot of people who aren't. So every candidate file is split in two.

The free half is enough to compare people like for like. Everyone in the list has the same six
numbers, worked out the same way, so a strong interview score from one candidate means the same
thing as from another. You don't need the name or the phone number to decide who's worth a closer
look.

When someone is, you unlock them. That gets you the resume, contact details, documents, the full
AI skill scorecard and the recording of the whole interview. Then you get in touch and carry on with
your own process. LetsHyre doesn't sit between you and the candidate after that.

<Callout tone="tip">
  Watch the recording before the first call. Ten minutes of the candidate
  explaining their own work will tell you which questions are worth asking, and
  which ones the interview has already answered.
</Callout>

## Why you pay per unlock, not per month

Most hiring tools charge a subscription, which suits a team that hires every week and punishes one
that hires twice a year. We went the other way. You pay only for the candidates you choose to meet.

A few details that matter when you buy:

- **Credits are a one-time purchase.** Nothing auto-renews, and credits don't expire. If you buy
  some in March and hire again in November, they're still there.
- **Every credit costs the same, whichever plan you pick.** Bigger plans give you more credits, more
  teammates who can see results, and more support. They don't unlock anything the smaller plans

- **Prices are in Indian rupees**, with GST added at checkout, and payment goes through Razorpay.
  The current plans are on the [pricing page](/en/pricing).

For professionals there's nothing to buy at all. LetsHyre is free from the first step to the last.

## Why a profile only lasts 90 days

This one surprises people, so it's worth explaining. A profile stays in the talent pool for 90 days,
and then it leaves.

<Figure
  src={ninety}
  srcDark={ninetyDark}
  alt="A timeline. From day 1 to day 90 the profile is in the talent pool and can be updated. After day 90 it leaves the pool. Later, the professional can come back with a new profile"
  width={720}
  height={250}
  caption="Ninety days in front of employers, then the pool makes room for people who are looking now."
/>

The reason is the employer's side of the table. A talent pool full of people who found a job six
months ago is worse than a small one, because every unlock spent on someone who's no longer looking
is wasted. Keeping profiles fresh means the people an employer finds are, as far as we can tell,
available.

If you're the candidate, you can update your profile while it's live. If your 90 days run out and
you're still looking, or looking again a year later, you can create a new profile and start over.

## What the AI does, and what it doesn't

We use AI where it saves people time: reading resumes, running the first interview, scoring it
against the role, and ranking candidates. We don't think it should make the decision.

<Callout tone="warning">
  The scorecards and insights are there to support a hiring decision, not to
  make it. They shouldn't be the only basis for hiring or rejecting anyone. The
  final decision always belongs to the employer, after they've met the person.
</Callout>

That's also why the recording is part of every unlock. A score is a summary, and summaries lose
things. If a number looks off, you can watch the interview and judge for yourself.
