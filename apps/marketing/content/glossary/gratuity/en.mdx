---
term: Gratuity
short: A lump sum your employer pays when you leave after five years of continuous service, or after one year on a fixed-term contract.
aliases: [gratuity payment]
---

Gratuity is a payment for long service, set by law. It's 15 days' wages for every completed year,
where wages means your last basic pay plus dearness allowance, and a month counts as 26 days. Five
years on a basic of ₹60,000 comes to about ₹1.73 lakh.

Permanent employees qualify after five years of continuous service. Since the labour codes took
effect in November 2025, fixed-term employees qualify after one year. Your employer has to pay it
within 30 days of it becoming due, and up to ₹20 lakh of it is tax-free for most private-sector
employees.

Many offers count gratuity inside the [CTC](/en/blogs/glossary/ctc), so it looks like salary even
though most people who leave before five years never receive it.
